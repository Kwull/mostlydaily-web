Why one missed day doesn't break a habit

A missed day feels like a failure when an app shows a streak going back to zero. It isn't one.

Habits form from repetition over weeks and months. A single gap in that repetition barely changes the pattern: the habit is still the default thing you do in that moment, at that place, after that cue. What changes the pattern is when one miss becomes two, then three, and the cue stops leading to the action.

The day after is the one that counts

That is why Mostly Daily's run is "never missed twice". One miss keeps the run going. Two misses in a row end it, gently, and a new one starts with your next check-in. The question after a missed day is never "did I ruin it?" but "what's the smallest thing I can do today?"

Make the smallest version count

Every habit can have a two-minute version: read one page, put on your shoes, floss one tooth. On a hard day it counts fully. It keeps the cue and the action connected, which is the part that matters.

Consistency, not perfection

Instead of a streak, each habit has a level that reflects the last couple of months. A miss after a good month costs a few points; you are still at Mostly. The level comes back as fast as it dipped. You never need 100%.
